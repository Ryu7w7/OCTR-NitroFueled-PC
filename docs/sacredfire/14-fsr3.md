# FSR 3 in CTR (v0.3 work, 2026-10-13)

bbport's temporal upscaler (`vk_temporal_upscaler`) is built around Bloodborne's frame: its
scene constants, its HDR scene color, its post-processing compute shader. None of that exists
in CTR, so CTR has its own, smaller path: `vk_ctr_fsr3.{h,cpp}` and
`host_shaders/ctr_fsr3_inputs.comp`. Setting `fsr3` (menu: *FSR 3 (experimental)*, off by
default), `fsr3_sharpness` (RCAS, 0 to 1).

## Where it runs

At the FSR 1 spot: the first draw of the display pass stretches the finished frame (render
size, RGBA8, tonemapped, before the HUD) into the display buffer. FSR 3.1 upscales that frame to
the display size instead; the display pass samples the result through a view with the game's
own format and swizzle (CTR reads its frame through a red/blue swizzle; without it the scene came
out blue). The HUD is drawn afterwards at display size and stays out of FSR. At render_res 1080
the sizes are equal and FSR 3 works as anti-aliasing.

## Inputs

- **G-buffer pass**: the pass with four color targets and depth. Color 3 is the velocity target
  (see below); the depth is the scene depth (D32S8, 2x MSAA; the velocity target is single
  sample). Its viewport gives the transform used for reprojection: `pixels = offset + scale *
  ndc`, `depth = zoffset + zscale * ndc z`. Measured: scale 640/-360, offset 640/360 at 720p,
  **zscale 0.8, zoffset 0** (not the 0.5/0.5 an OpenGL-style projection suggests).
- **Camera**: the first G-buffer draw's vertex constants (528 bytes): floats 32-47 the view,
  48-63 the projection (column-major, OpenGL style: x 0.75, y 1.333, A -1.00021, B -32.0068,
  so near 16 and far about 150000, vertical FOV 73.7 degrees), 64-79 **the previous frame's
  view** (checked: equal to the view of the frame before), 0-15 the draw's world-view-projection.
- **Depth**: sample 0 copied to R32F.
- **Motion** (pixels, previous minus current, no jitter):
  - the world: reprojected from the depth with `projection * previous view * inverse(view)`.
    Checked on three consecutive dumped frames: warping the previous frame with it lowers the
    error from 10.8 to 6.6 (mean absolute luminance), and scaling the vectors by 0.8-1.2 or
    shifting them by half a pixel only makes it worse;
  - moving objects: the velocity target marks them in memory channel 2 (255) and stores, in
    channels 1 and 0 around 127, the object's own motion without the camera's (the world is 127
    everywhere even at full speed). On the player's kart no decoding of it (linear or squared,
    either channel order, 0.4 to 0.6 pixels a step, added to the camera's motion) matched the
    frames as well as leaving the kart still on the screen (5.6 against 7.4 at best): the camera
    follows it. Still on the screen is the default; `BB_FSR3_MV` tries decodings.
- **Jitter**: Halton(2,3), 8 x (output/render)^2 phases (18 at 720p to 1080p), applied as a
  sub-pixel viewport offset to draws into the scene depth that are not full-screen passes.

## Diagnostics

| Variable | Effect |
|---|---|
| `BB_FSR3=1` | turns FSR 3 on (the setting otherwise) |
| `BB_FSR3_DEBUG=1/2/3` | shows the motion / depth / raw velocity target instead of the frame |
| `BB_FSR3_DUMP_TRIGGER=<file>` | creating the file saves the next `BB_FSR3_DUMP_FRAMES` (3) frames' inputs and the frame to `BB_FSR3_DUMP_DIR` and logs the camera constants and viewport |
| `BB_FSR3_MV=sx,sy,bias,mode,cx,cy` | decodes moving objects (mode 1 linear, 2 squared) and adds them; `nocamera` drops the world motion |
| `BB_FSR3_NO_JITTER`, `BB_FSR3_JITTER_FLIP`, `BB_FSR3_DEPTH_INVERTED` | tests |

## Fixed on the way

- The depth and velocity targets differ in sample count: both are sampled separately.
- The texture cache can delete the G-buffer's images between the G-buffer pass and the display
  pass (loading screens reuse their memory). Using the stale ids crashed the game (heap
  corruption) while it loaded the characters; the ids are now checked with their uids.

## DLSS (2026-10-13)

`temporal=dlss` (menu and launcher: *Temporal upscaling*, NVIDIA DLSS) feeds NVIDIA DLSS the same
inputs: the frame, the R32F depth, the pixel motion and the jitter, through
`sacredfire_dlss.dll` (`dlss_bridge/`, IFreemz's MIT bridge with this project's NGX id) and
NVIDIA's `nvngx_dlss.dll` next to `sacredfire.exe`. `vk_dlss_ngx` (from the same project) adds the
Vulkan instance and device extensions NGX asks for and initializes it after the device. Without
the two DLLs, on other GPUs or when NGX fails, FSR 3 runs instead (the log says why). Quality
mode follows the scale (Quality at 1.5x, DLAA at 1x); `BB_DLSS_PRESET` picks a model preset,
`BB_DLSS_JITTER_FLIP` tests the jitter sign, `BB_DLSS=0` skips the bridge.

Tested on an RTX 3070 (1280x720 to 1920x1080, Crash Cove): DLSS, FSR 3 and off switch live from
the menu; DLSS looked best of the three, no trails or shimmer.
